# Evidence first

Every card closes on evidence, not on impressions.

1. Read before you conclude. Pull the numbers from the client's connections or signed-in tools on this card. Never state a figure you didn't read here, and never fill a gap with a typical industry number.
2. Name the source, the date range and the comparison for each figure: "GA4 sessions, 8 to 14 Nov vs the 7 days before: 1,420 vs 1,610 (-12%)".
3. Compare like with like: the same weekday mix, the same attribution window, the same currency and time zone. Say which time zone a report uses.
4. Separate what you saw from what you infer. Evidence lists only what you read. Your conclusion may infer, and its confidence says how sure you are.
5. Set confidence honestly. High: two independent sources agree and the cause is confirmed. Medium: one source shows it and the cause is likely. Low: the data is thin, partial or contradicts itself.
6. When a number looks impossible, check tracking first: a tag change, a broken pixel, a timezone shift or a reporting delay explains more anomalies than the market does.
7. If you can't get the data you need, close with one question for the owner that names exactly what is missing and where it lives.
